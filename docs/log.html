Aug 2026

Pipeline built & validated against real land cover

725 Sentinel-2 chips across the Boulder–Denver Front Range, each encoded by Clay v1.5 into a 1024-dim vector, then checked against ground truth rather than taken on faith.

82.3%
variance in top-3 PCA components
0.275
Adjusted Rand Index vs. ESA WorldCover
725 / 725
chips matched to a WorldCover class

The dominant PCA axis (PC1, 59.4% of variance alone) turned out to track human development rather than vegetation cleanly — negatively correlated with NDVI (−0.55), positively with NDBI/built-up-ness (0.61). Unsupervised KMeans clusters lined up with real ESA WorldCover land cover well above chance: one cluster came out almost entirely “Built-up,” another almost entirely “Tree cover,” while cropland and grassland — spectrally similar in a single snapshot — bled across several clusters instead of separating cleanly.

Honest loose end: querying the chip nearest the Marshall Fire burn scar (Superior/Louisville, Dec 2021) didn't return visually “burned” neighbors — just generic suburban/grassland edge. By the 2024 imagery used here the scar is ~2.5 years regrown, so this doesn't confirm or rule out whether Clay retains a burn signature that long. That's the seed for a proper before/after comparison later in this log.

Aug 2026

Embedding arithmetic: urban − vegetation + water

The classic word2vec trick (king − man + woman ≈ queen) applied to satellite chips, to test something stronger than “these chips cluster together”: is the embedding space linearly structured, such that concepts can be added and subtracted like vectors? Runs entirely against the embeddings already committed in docs/data/ — no Colab, no GPU, just analysis/embedding_arithmetic.js.

Concept anchors were built from what the previous entry already validated: urban = mean embedding of the ~pure “Built-up” cluster, vegetation = mean embedding of the ~pure “Tree cover” cluster, water = the single chip nearest Boulder Reservoir (water chips are too sparse here to form their own cluster).

0.951
top-1 cosine similarity to the result vector
0.828
mean similarity across all other chips (baseline)

The top result is meaningfully above the baseline, not just close to everything — the arithmetic is doing something real. The closest chips (mostly from a cluster that WorldCover confirms is a Built-up/Grassland mix, not the pure “Built-up” cluster used as the urban anchor) sit at moderate NDVI (~0.16–0.22), scattered across the eastern plains near Denver. Read as a sentence: “urban character, minus the forest influence, plus a water-adjacent quality” lands on development-in-open-grassland-near-water — not dense downtown, not forest, which is exactly the kind of place that description fits on this actual landscape (the Front Range's development sprawls into prairie to the east, not into the forested foothills to the west).
